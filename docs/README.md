# OctoAcme Project Management Processes

OctoAcme manages cross-functional work through an iterative delivery lifecycle that begins with initiation and ends with retrospective learning. At the start of a project, the team validates the business need, defines success metrics, identifies stakeholders and dependencies, and creates a lightweight project one-pager. This helps determine whether work should move forward into planning. The key decision gate is whether the problem is well defined, stakeholders agree on priority, and the team has capacity to deliver. This early stage establishes the foundation for scope, ownership, and measurable outcomes.

Once an initiative is approved, the team turns it into a concrete plan. Backlog items are prioritized, estimated, and linked to clear acceptance criteria, a Definition of Done, and milestone-based timing. Risks and dependencies are captured in a risk register, cross-team needs are called out, and the project plan is aligned across stakeholders and delivery teams. This planning phase reduces ambiguity and ensures the team is working on the right problems with realistic expectations and ownership.

Execution focuses on disciplined, visible delivery. The team uses a project board to track work, holds regular standups and syncs, reviews progress in demos, and escalates blockers in a clear sequence from the team to the PM, Product Lead, and sponsor when needed. Pull requests are expected to be small, clearly scoped, and accompanied by issue links, acceptance criteria, and successful CI checks before review and merge. Quality is protected through testing, linting, security checks, and manual QA where needed. The project manager and product lead coordinate delivery cadence, stakeholder communication, and risk management throughout this phase.

OctoAcme also emphasizes structured release and continuous improvement. Before deployment, teams confirm quality, documentation, release notes, rollback readiness, and smoke test coverage. After release, the team verifies the changes, communicates outcomes to stakeholders and support, and logs any issues. Retrospectives after major milestones, releases, or incidents capture what went well, what could improve, and the actions needed to address it. This creates a continuous learning loop where improvements are owned, reviewed, and tracked as part of the project backlog.

## Core Roles

- Project Manager (PM): coordinates delivery, schedules, risks, communications, and project artifacts.
- Product Manager (PdM): defines the problem, prioritizes the roadmap, and measures value.
- Developers: design, build, test, review, and maintain software.
- QA/Testing: validate quality, acceptance criteria, and release readiness.
- Stakeholders: provide input, approvals, and strategic alignment.
